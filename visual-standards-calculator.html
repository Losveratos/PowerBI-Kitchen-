Visual standards calculator: Paid, Flexible or MacGyver?

What does it cost to implement visual standards in Power BI with 3rd-party visuals, open source, Deneb or core visuals? The guiding principle: standard first, only as bespoke as necessary. So the calculator first checks whether there is a professional reason to depart from the core visuals, and then compares across three levels: Overview with three inputs, Narrow down via requirements and red flags, Details with every assumption and source. It gives an indication with ranges, not a price quote: every assumption is editable and marked with its evidence. If this is your first time here, answer seven short questions in the guided start — it only sets fields that you will find again, every one of them, in levels 2 and 3.

    Self-service share of report authors (hybrid)
    Who is allowed to build standard reports? The operating model often shifts more cost than the choice of tool: it determines how many report authors are trained in depth, how many chart variants appear and how much governance is needed.
    If you stay with today's approach, the switch costs €0. Every other approach carries the rebuild of the existing estate.
    Reports on today's approach that would be rebuilt on a switch.

    Four fine adjustments have moved to level 3: semantic models, adoption rate (standard compliance), uncertainty in the volume assumptions and the hours anchor. They are not gone, just one level deeper — their defaults still count in unchanged.

    A custom visual is registered once in the tenant. A DAX UDF, by contrast, sits in functions.tmdl in the definition folder of every semantic model and has to be rolled out and maintained there one by one: twenty models mean twenty libraries. A factor on setup and initial build.
    Adoption rate (standard compliance)
    Share of newly built charts that actually follow the standard. The rest is built ad hoc and costed at the rates of the do-nothing option (hours per chart, maintenance rate, follow-up queries). Default 100%; the suggested values (85 / 80 / 70 / 65%) are an authors' estimate with no source. Watch the direction: in the model a low adoption rate makes paid and open source more expensive above all — their standard hours are below the ad-hoc rate, so every workaround costs more there. With core + SVG the ad-hoc rate is below the standard rate, so in the model going around the standard is almost free. The reason is the same as with the do-nothing option: the loss of value from inconsistent charts is not priced in. Adjustable per approach in level 3.
    Uncertainty in the volume assumptions
    Draws report authors, viewers, reports, charts per report and chart types per scenario via PERT around your inputs — paired, so the same volume applies to all four approaches in each scenario. The P10–P90 band gets wider, the ranking stays stable. Expected value, do-nothing option and every chart that uses them stay on your inputs. The order of magnitude is the real message: ±20% on the volume assumptions widens the band by only about 1 to 2%. The uncertainty in this calculator sits almost entirely in the assumptions, not in your volume assumptions — if you want to get more precise, measure hours, not reports.

    Result and assessment

    Total cost over the horizon, cost per viewer per year, upfront investment against running cost. The ranking is an indication under uncertainty in the inputs, not a price quote.     Reading guide: four blocks, in this order click to jump there

    If you are seeing the result for the first time, read it from top to bottom in this order. Clicking a row jumps to that block and highlights it briefly.

    Key terms explained
    P10 / P50 / P90
    In 10%, 50% and 90% of the 1,500 simulation runs the cost was below this value. P50 is the “middle” number, P10 to P90 the range you should plan with.
    PERT
    A three-point estimate (min, likely, max) from which a probability distribution is formed. The expected value is (min + 4·likely + max) / 6.
    MoSCoW
    Must (cannot do without it), should (important), could (nice to have), won't (not this time). A must with fulfilment level 1 is a knock-out.
    Knock-out
    The approach is excluded but stays visible with its cost, so that the price of the constraint can be seen.
    Net present value (NPV)
    Later payments discounted at the discount rate. Over 10 years it makes a difference, over 3 hardly any.
    Volume tiering
    Volume discount on the licence: the more users, the cheaper the individual seat. A negotiated vendor quote is calculated without tiering, because the discount is already in it.
    Red flag
    A hard constraint from the tenant, IT security or procurement that rules out an approach before anything is calculated.
    Price anchor
    The assumed list price per licensed user per year, as long as there is no real quote. The licensed headcount is all report authors plus the share of viewers with access to standard reports, not all viewers. It flips the ranking more than any other assumption.

    These ranges come out of the chosen model assumptions. They are not an empirically validated forecast of actual project cost. The ranking holds only for the scope described (standard charts, no data model, no data preparation), the product families considered and the constraints shown. If you change an assumption, the badge still shows where the original value came from.
    Ranking: cost ⟷ requirements met
    100% = ranking by cost alone. Move it to the left to give weight to soft benefits (should/could fulfilment) as well. Weighted-scoring caveat: weights are subjective, which is why they are visible and adjustable.
    Perspective of the charts and key figures
    “Per user per year” divides every amount by the selected user base and by the horizon. The break-even then shows how far build-out cost spreads across the number of viewers.
    Decision measure
    Ranking, headline, key figures and the overview sentence all use this quantile. Ranking by P90 = conservative: the approach with the wider distribution loses its lead. As measured, the order only flips in the corporate preset (as of v0.19: by P50 and P75 Deneb leads, by P90 paid does — Deneb's wider distribution costs it the lead there); in the other four presets only the gap changes.
    Draw assumptions paired across approaches
    Paired: an optimistic hours assumption applies to all four approaches at once in each scenario, so differences are comparable pairwise. Independent: every approach draws its own random values, and then part of the difference comes from the sampling.

    What these numbers are

    Indications under uncertainty. Not a quote, not a business case, not a price statement. The one exception: numbers from a vendor quote that you entered yourself in level 2 — those are as solid as your quote.

    Where the assumptions come from

    …

    The assumptions the ranking hangs on — hours per chart, per approach — are among the unevidenced ones. We found no solid literature for them; the values come from a simulated expert panel and from our own experience. The evidence bar shows, per approach, how much of the cost depends on them.

    Six known gaps from an independent review

    An independent review of the model (09/2026) found seven causal chains that shift the result noticeably and that the calculator either does not know or gets wrong. One of them has been fixed: since v0.18 the licence volume knows about the report classes. Six are still open.

    Four of the six work in favour of the approaches we stand for ourselves. So the model is not neutral; it has a known bias in our own direction. The full review report, including suggestions for modelling these gaps, is openly available in the repository.

    What is not included

    • Power BI base licences (Pro, PPU, the base price of a capacity) — they apply equally to all four approaches.
    • Paginated reports (RDL) — Power BI custom visuals do not exist there at all, neither purchased ones nor Deneb. A printed report pack gets built a second time in every approach.
    • The benefit side — only as a requirements score and as “€ per requirements point”, not as a return.
    • Tax questions (e.g. German withholding tax under § 50a EStG on licences from foreign vendors). Capitalisation and amortisation are included as orientation, not as accounting or tax advice.

    And the most important point

    The tipping point — the core number of this calculator — has moved by more than 30% twice since v0.13, both times through a correction to the model, not through new data. The direction of the statements is robust; the exact number is not. If you use it as a decision threshold, recompute it with your own numbers instead of copying it from here.

    Sources, assumptions, method

    Honesty note: For “hours per chart” there is no solid figure anywhere in the literature or in blogs. The defaults for build-out, reuse, setup, training, maintenance, breaking updates and user support come from a simulated expert panel (20 role profiles from consultant to internal audit, estimated independently by a language model, median per value; raw data in the repository under sessions/). That is a structured opinion, not a measurement, and it is the model's most important lever. Your own experience belongs in here. Figures such as “46% faster” or “10x” were deliberately not adopted as defaults, because they conflate reading speed with build time; the “46%”, incidentally, comes from the study “More than just a standard” (blueforte / TU Munich 2019) on the IBCS standard, and not from a visual vendor. Two assumptions still live only in the code and appear in no table of assumptions: the normalisation mix of the chart mix (cx0 = 50 / 35 / 15 — the mix that the panel hours per chart apply to) and the learning-curve anchor (“the panel value is the 10th chart per report author”). Both are noticeable: move the anchor from 10 to 20 and the hours for the code-based approaches rise by around 18%; assume a panel mix of 40 / 35 / 25 instead of 50 / 35 / 15 and the complexity factor for core drops to 0.78. Prices come mostly from search snippets of the vendors' own pages (as of 09/2026) and are to be checked with the vendor before any decision.
    Limits of the comparison: Paginated reports (RDL) are not included. Power BI custom visuals do not exist there — neither purchased ones, nor Deneb, nor a core visual taken from the report. If you print a report pack or send it out pixel-perfect, you build it a second time in RDL, and equally so in all four approaches; the calculator does not reflect that second build in any approach. The do-nothing option is open in the same way: an independent blind estimate (09/2026, with no knowledge of the values set here) arrived at a maintenance rate of 15 · 30 · 50% (here 20 · 30 · 45), 3 · 8 · 15 minutes of reader time per viewer per week (here 0.5 · 2 · 6) and 40 · 120 · 300 hours of follow-up queries per 100 viewers per year (here 6 · 12 · 20). All three of the higher values would support the claim that “a standard pays off” — which is precisely why they appear here as a range and not in the defaults.
